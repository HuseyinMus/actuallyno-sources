# Video 4: Wet hair, cold?

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short **"Wet hair, cold?"**. Each claim in the script is listed with its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen.

Checked 2026-10-25. Health wording: "may", "linked to"; never "causes" or "proven". Numbers come from the paper, not media coverage (the "29% vs 9%" figure is not used).

## 1. "Your mom said it. Doctors called it a myth." (0–3 s)
- **Source:** Eccles R., "Acute cooling of the body surface and the common cold", *Rhinology* 40, 109–114 (2002), PMID 12357708.
- **Wording:** the belief that a cold follows a chill, "especially as the result of wet clothes and hair"; "Present scientific opinion dismisses any cause-and-effect relationship between acute cooling of the body surface and common cold."

## 2. "A cold is caused by a virus." (3–12 s)
- **Source:** CDC, "About Common Cold": "More than 200 respiratory viruses can cause colds"; "Rhinoviruses are the most frequent cause of colds". <https://www.cdc.gov/common-cold/about/index.html>

## 3. The Cardiff study (12–28 s)
- **Source:** Johnson C. & Eccles R., "Acute cooling of the feet and the onset of common cold symptoms", *Family Practice* 22(6), 608–613 (2005). doi:10.1093/fampra/cmi072, PMID 16286463 (abstract via Europe PMC).
- **Wording:** "180 healthy subjects were randomized to receive either a foot chill or control procedure"; "13/90 subjects who were chilled reported they were suffering from a cold in the 4/5 days after the procedure compared to 5/90 control subjects (P=0.047)"; conclusion: "around 10% of subjects who are chilled"; "Further studies are needed".
- **Checks:** (a) the colds were **self-reported** symptoms, not lab-confirmed infections. (b) The abstract gives no water temperature; "bare feet in cold water for 20 minutes" comes from press coverage of the study (e.g. WIS-TV/AP, 2005), and the "empty bowl" control could not be confirmed from a primary page (the journal site blocks automated readers). "Ice water" is not supported. (c) "Five days later" → the paper says "in the 4/5 days after".
- **On screen:** `Johnson & Eccles, Family Practice 2005 · 13/90 vs 5/90 · self-reported`

## 4. Mechanism (28–40 s)
- **Source:** Eccles 2002 (above): a **hypothesis** that cooling "causes reflex vasoconstriction in the nose and upper airways, and that this vasoconstrictor response may inhibit respiratory defence … by converting an asymptomatic subclinical viral infection into a symptomatic clinical infection."
- **Check:** not measured in the 2005 trial. Narration must say "may".
- **On screen:** `hypothesis · Eccles, Rhinology 2002`

## 5. "Study tested cold feet, not wet hair" (40–46 s)
- True of the 2005 trial (foot chilling only). Shown on screen as required.
