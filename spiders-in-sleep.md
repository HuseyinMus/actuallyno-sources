# Video 10: Do you swallow spiders in your sleep?

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short on the "8 spiders a year" claim. Each claim in the script is listed with its source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen. Checked 2026-10-31.

## 1. The claim: "8 spiders a year in your sleep"
- **Source:** Annie Sneed, "Fact or Fiction?: People Swallow 8 Spiders a Year While They Sleep", *Scientific American*, 15 April 2014. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/fact-or-fiction-people-swallow-8-spiders-a-year-while-they-sleep1/
- **Verdict, in its words:** the claim "isn't true. Not even close."
- **Other versions:** the Burke Museum page gives the myth as "an average of four live spiders in your sleep each year" and notes "another common version is that you swallow an average of 20 in your lifetime". The film uses 8 because it is the version people repeat most. It is shown as the claim, not as a number we back.
- **On screen:** `Scientific American, 2014`

## 2. A sleeping human is a warning sign to a spider
- **Source:** Scientific American 2014 (above), quoting Rod Crawford, arachnid curator at the Burke Museum, Seattle.
- **Wording:**
  - "A slumbering person breathes, has a beating heart and perhaps snores—all of which create vibrations that warn spiders of danger."
  - Crawford: "A sleeping person is not something a spider would willingly approach."
- **Check:** the draft said "breathing, heartbeat, rolling over". The source lists breathing, heartbeat and **snoring**, so the film uses those three. "Earthquake" is our metaphor for those vibrations, not a word from the source.
- **On screen:** `Rod Crawford, Burke Museum · via Scientific American`

## 3. No case on record
- **Source:** Rod Crawford, Spider Myths, Burke Museum. "Myth: You swallow spiders in your sleep". https://www.burkemuseum.org/collections-and-research/biology/arachnology-and-entomology/spider-myths/myth-you-swallow-spiders (older version, text © 2003–2008, updated 2 Sep 2010: https://burkemuseum.org/static/spidermyth/myths/whileyousleep.html)
- **Wording:**
  - "No such case is on formal record anywhere in scientific or medical literature."
  - "This very widespread urban legend has no basis in fact."
- **Supporting:** Scientific American says people who report it "never seem to have any concrete evidence" and notes "a sore lack of eyewitnesses".
- **Check:** "no case has ever been recorded" is stronger than the source. The film says **"no case on record"** in science or medicine. We do not say it has never happened.
- **On screen:** `Burke Museum: no case on record`

## 4. The origin story: Lisa Birgit Holst
- **Source:** Snopes, "Lisa Birgit Holst" (Snopes' own explanation page). https://www.snopes.com/lisa-birgit-holst/
- **Wording:**
  - "'PC Professional' and columnist Lisa Birgit Holst do not exist."
  - The name "is an anagram of the phrase 'This is a big troll.'"
  - "This 'easter egg' was created over 20 years ago in April of 2001 as wink to our long-time readers".
  - The 1954 book *Insect Fact and Folklore* is real, but page 24 "says nothing about the 'people swallow eight spiders per year' rumor".
- **What this means:** in 2001 Snopes' own spider article gave Holst's column as the source. Snopes now says that origin was its own planted joke.
- **Anagram check (ours):** "LISA BIRGIT HOLST" and "THIS IS A BIG TROLL" both use exactly these 15 letters: A B G H I I I L L O R S S T T.
- **Not used:** the "1993" date of the supposed column. The Snopes page does not give it; we only found it in retellings, so the film shows no year on the fake magazine.

## 5. The rumor is older than the fake origin
- **Source:** Snopes, same page: "this urban legend pre-dated our own debunking", citing a 1999 list "50 Top Pieces of Trivia" and "We're still on the hunt for the origins of this long-standing rumor."
- **Check:** the film must not say Snopes invented the spider claim. Snopes invented only the origin story. The real origin is unknown.
- **On screen:** `Snopes: Holst story was a 2001 "easter egg"`
