# Video 5: Shamonda: the first documented human case

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short **"Shamonda: the first documented human case"**. Each claim in the script is listed with its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen.

## 1. "Never known to infect a human" / "used to judge a similar virus safe" (0–7 s)
- **Source:** ECDC, *Risk assessment: New orthobunyavirus isolated from infected cattle and small livestock, potential implications for human health*, Stockholm, Nov/Dec 2011 (Schmallenberg virus). <https://ecdc.europa.eu/sites/portal/files/media/en/publications/Publications/231112_TER_Risk_assessment_Schmallenberg_virus.pdf>
- **Wording (read from the PDF):** "the new virus is closest to the following Simbu serogroup viruses: Shamonda-, Aino- and Akabane-viruses, which do not cause disease in humans"; "the genetically most related viruses (Shamonda-, Aino-, and Akabane-viruses) do not cause disease in humans."
- **Follow-up:** joint ECDC/RIVM/RKI rapid risk assessment, May 2012: zoonotic potential of SBV "absent or very low". <https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/sites/default/files/media/en/publications/Publications/TER-Joint-ECDC-RIVM-RKI-Rapid-Risk-Assessment-Schmallenberg-virus-May-2012.pdf>
- **Check:** ECDC judged human SBV infection "unlikely but cannot be excluded". It used Shamonda as an argument, not as "proof". Narration: "pointed to it".
- **On screen:** `ECDC risk assessment, 2011`

## 2. Nigeria 1960s, Japan, hosts, midges (7–15 s)
- **Source:** WHO Q&A "Shamonda virus": "first isolated from cattle in Nigeria in 1965"; reported in animals in African countries and Japan; hosts cattle, sheep, goats, horses; spread thought to be by *Culicoides* midges. <https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/shamonda-virus>
- **Source:** Causey et al. 1972, isolations of Simbu-group viruses in Ibadan, Nigeria 1964–69 (cattle blood, *Culicoides*). Cited in EID 2026 (below).
- **Source:** Yanase et al., *Arch. Virol.* 2005; NIAH Japan 2004: isolates in 2002 from *Culicoides* and sentinel cattle, Kagoshima and Miyazaki. <https://www.naro.go.jp/english/niah/researchtopics/2004/043244.html>
- **Check:** the brief's timeline says "Japan → 2011". Japan is **2002**; 2011 is the ECDC note. The timeline reads Nigeria 1965 → Japan 2002 → ECDC 2011 → Europe Jun 2026 → France Oct 2026.
- **On screen:** `WHO · Yanase et al. 2005`

## 3. Europe 2026 (15–25 s)
- **Source:** WHO Q&A (above): "first detected in Europe in June 2026, in cattle in southern Germany and Switzerland", later Austria, Belgium, France.
- **Source:** Emerging Infectious Diseases 32(12), Dec 2026 (early release), "Emergence of Shamonda Orthobunyavirus, Central Europe, 2026": disease reported "since mid-June 2026" in southern Germany and Switzerland; ">100 affected farms" in Baden-Württemberg by end of July. <https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/32/12/26-1496_article>
- **Source:** Euronews, 23 Oct 2026 (ECDC interview): animal detections this summer in 12 countries plus Jersey: Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Portugal, Spain, Switzerland. <https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/23/shamonda-virus-first-human-case-is-an-alert-not-a-reason-to-panic-says-eu-health-agency>
- **Checks:** (a) Europe starts in **June**, not July. (b) It is 12 countries **in all**, not "a dozen more". (c) Not uniformly notifiable, so the map shows the order countries appear in reports, not exact dates for each.
- **On screen:** `WHO · EID 2026 · ECDC`

## 4. The human case (25–38 s)
- **Source:** French Ministry of Agriculture / Government statement, 22 Oct 2026, quoted by franceinfo and Euronews (the original communiqué text was not found online; press quotes only):
  - "un patient présentant une immunodépression sévère"; "cas exceptionnel"
  - hypothesis of "contamination vectorielle… par la piqûre d'un moucheron de type Culicoïde dans l'est de la France"
  - "Des investigations cliniques, biologiques et épidémiologiques sont encore en cours"
  - "aucun risque sanitaire pour la population générale n'est identifié à ce jour"
  - <https://www.franceinfo.fr/sante/maladie/un-premier-cas-de-virus-shamonda-chez-l-etre-humain-detecte-en-france_8248633.html>
  - <https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/22/france-confirms-first-human-case-of-shamonda-virus-previously-found-only-in-animals>
- **Details:** the patient was treated at Hospices Civils de Lyon and died in early October. Euronews calls it "the first documented and reported detection of the virus in a human". WHO: "first documented human disease".
- **Checks:** (a) Infection was likely in eastern France, not Lyon itself; the patient was treated in Lyon. (b) Whether the virus caused or contributed to the death is still under investigation. Narration must not say it killed the patient.
- **On screen:** `French Ministries of Health & Agriculture, 22 Oct 2026`

## 6. Visual details (video 5)
- **Virus drawing:** an illustration, not a micrograph. It is labelled "illustration · virus particle ≈ 100 nm". Orthobunyaviruses are enveloped spheres of about 80–120 nm with surface spikes and a three-segment RNA genome. This is textbook knowledge and was not re-checked against a primary page this session.
- **Midge:** *Culicoides* are "the smallest (1–3 mm)" of the blood-feeding flies (checklist of Ecuadorian *Culicoides*, 2023, <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9879131/>). Extension guides give the same range and note spotted wings in most species, e.g. UF/IFAS EDIS "Biting midges, no-see-ums" <https://journals.flvc.org/edis/article/view/120996>. The spotted wings are drawn in the genus's usual pattern; the species involved in this case is "still unknown" (ECDC via Euronews, 23 Oct 2026).
- **Maps:** Natural Earth 1:50m country outlines (public domain), via world-atlas 2.0.2. Countries are lit in the order they appear in reports; exact dates for each country are not claimed.
- **Patient:** shown only as an abstract silhouette without a face.
