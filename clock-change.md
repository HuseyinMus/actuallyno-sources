# Video 1: Daylight saving time: do clock changes save energy?

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short **"Daylight saving time: do clock changes save energy?"**. Each claim in the script is listed with its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen.

Checked 2026-10-24. Each claim in the script, its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short on-screen credit.

## 1. "This Sunday, millions of people change their clocks" (0–4 s)
Script changed from "Tomorrow" on 2026-10-24 (user decision) so the line holds for the whole weekend.
- **Source:** Directive 2000/84/EC (summer-time arrangements), as summarised by the European Commission, DG MOVE: <https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/summertime_en>
- **Wording:** Member States "switch back to their standard time ("winter-time") on the last Sunday of October."
- **Check:** The last Sunday of October 2026 is **25 October 2026**. "Tomorrow" is true **only if the Short goes out on 24 October 2026**. The US changes on 1 November 2026 (first Sunday of November, Energy Policy Act of 2005).
- **On screen:** `EU Directive 2000/84/EC`

## 2. "That was the whole idea: more daylight, less electricity" (3–12 s)
- **Source:** Kotchen & Grant, NBER Working Paper 14429 (Oct 2008), abstract and p. 1: <https://www.nber.org/papers/w14429>
- **Wording:** "the primary rationale for DST has always been to promote energy conservation"; Germany's first DST aimed at "reducing demand for electrical lighting".
- **On screen:** `Kotchen & Grant, NBER w14429`

## 3. Indiana statewide in 2006 / "researchers checked the meters" (12–20 s)
- **Source:** same paper, pp. 3–4 and 8: <https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14429/w14429.pdf>
- **Wording:** "a state law that required all counties to begin practicing DST in 2006"; "more than 7 million observations on monthly billing data for the vast majority of households in southern Indiana" (data from Duke Energy meter reads, 2004–2006).
- **On screen:** `7M+ monthly meter bills · Duke Energy, 2004–06`

## 4. "Home electricity use went up, not down" (20–30 s)
- **Source:** same paper, abstract and Table 4.
- **Wording:** "DST increases residential electricity demand. Estimates of the overall increase are approximately 1 percent"; in the fall "estimates range between 2 and 4 percent". Table 4, model (a): **+0.96 %** (range across models 0.80–1.03 %). Cost: "$9 million per year" to Indiana households.
- **On screen:** `+1% home electricity · fall +2–4%`

## 5. "Lights saved a little; air conditioning and heating ate it back" (30–38 s)
- **Source:** same paper, abstract and Table 7 (a simulation for southern Indiana, 2006, not the meter data).
- **Wording:** "a tradeoff between reducing demand for lighting and increasing demand for heating and cooling". Table 7 overall: lighting −4.5, cooling +9.1, heating +1.7 (the paper's units for these columns are printed as kWh/day; drawn on screen only as relative bar lengths and labelled "simulation").
- **On screen:** `Simulation, Kotchen & Grant Table 7`

## 6. "The European Parliament voted to end clock changes in 2019… and it still hasn't happened" (34–40 s)
Script changed from "The EU voted" on 2026-10-24 (user decision): only Parliament voted; the Council has no position.
- **Source A:** European Parliament press release, 26 March 2019: <https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20190321IPR32107/parliament-backs-proposal-to-end-switch-between-summer-and-winter-time-in-2021>
- **Wording:** "410 in favour, 192 against, 51 abstentions"; "The text adopted is the Parliament's position for negotiations with EU ministers".
- **Source B:** European Parliament Legislative Train, "Discontinuing seasonal changes of time": <https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-union-of-democratic-change/file-discontinuing-seasonal-changes-of-time>
- **Wording:** status **"Blocked"** (as of 20/09/2026), blocking institution: Council.
- **Check:** It was the **European Parliament** that voted, not the EU as a whole; the Council never adopted a position. See the open question in the brief.
- **On screen:** `European Parliament, 26 Mar 2019 · 410–192` / `Status: blocked in Council (EP Legislative Train, Sep 2026)`

## Thumbnails (checked 2026-10-24)
- **v1 "IT USES MORE?"**: claim 4 above (home electricity +~1 %, Indiana). The meter has no scale on purpose: a 1 % change drawn as a big spike would exaggerate it.
- **v3 "SAVES ENERGY ✓ → ACTUALLY, NO."**: claim 4 above; the small note on the thumbnail limits it to "Indiana, 2006: home use went UP". The study covers residential electricity in southern Indiana, not all energy everywhere.
- **v2 Franklin**: Founders Online, "Aux Auteurs du Journal de Paris" (printed in the Journal de Paris, 26 April 1784): <https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Franklin/01-42-02-0063>. The editors' note: the letter "is best known for its satire and the suggestion of rising with the sun, which in our time has led readers to conclude, falsely, that he invented Daylight Saving Time." His proposals were to tax shutters, ration candles, ring church bells and fire cannon at sunrise; he never proposed changing clocks. Clock-shifting was proposed by William Willett (1907); Germany first adopted DST in 1916 (Kotchen & Grant, NBER w14429, p. 1). So "BLAME HIM?" is misleading; v2 uses "IT WAS A JOKE." instead (`v2-blame-him.jpg` kept only for comparison). The film itself does not mention Franklin.
