# Video 2: Spoon or no spoon? Microplastics in the brain

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short **"Spoon or no spoon? Microplastics in the brain"**. Each claim in the script is listed with its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen.

Checked 2026-10-24. Health topic: every claim is worded as "found", "linked" or "suggests", never "proven".

## 1. "In 2025, a study measured plastic in human brains" (3–10 s)
- **Source:** Nihart A. J. et al., "Bioaccumulation of microplastics in decedent human brains", *Nature Medicine* 31, 1114–1119 (published online 3 Feb 2025). <https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03453-1>
- **Wording (p. 3):** brain samples had a median of "3345 µg g−1 … in 2016 samples and 4917 µg g−1 (25–75%: 4,026–5,608 µg g−1) in 2024 samples"; method: pyrolysis gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (Py-GC/MS); polyethylene was the dominant polymer.
- **"About 7 grams. One spoon":** not in the paper. It is an extrapolation: 4,917 µg/g × ~1.4 kg brain ≈ 6.9 g (the same arithmetic as R&D World, "Does your brain really have enough plastic in it to make a spoon?"). It assumes the frontal-cortex median holds for the whole brain. On screen it is labelled "if the median held for the whole brain".
- **On screen:** `Nihart et al., Nature Medicine, Feb 2025`

## 2. "Then chemists looked at the method" / "the test" (15–30 s)
- **Source:** Monikh F. A. et al., "Challenges in studying microplastics in human brain", Matters Arising, *Nature Medicine* 31(12), 4034–4035 (2025). DOI 10.1038/s41591-025-04045-3. (The brief said "Nature 2025": it is *Nature Medicine*.)
- **Points:** KOH digestion is "suboptimal for lipid-rich tissues such as the brain"; Py-GC/MS "is not a selective detection method for PE if the particles are part of a matrix, rich in organic matter, containing lipids"; "limited contamination controls and lack of validation steps".
- **Also:** German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR), "Microplastics in the brain": the mass values "must be considered unrealistically high due to the reasonable assumption of false positive assignments". <https://www.bfr.bund.de/en/notification/microplastics-in-the-brain/>
- **The authors' side (Nihart et al.):** they note lipids "have been noted as a potential source of interference" and chose KOH digestion to avoid losing plastic with the lipids.
- **On screen:** `Monikh et al., Nature Medicine Matters Arising, 2025`

## 3. "Your brain is 60% fat" (25–32 s)
- **Source:** O'Brien J. S. & Sampson E. L., "Lipid composition of the normal human brain", *J. Lipid Res.* 6, 537–544 (1965). <https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-2275(20)39619-x>
- **Wording:** lipid is 36–40 % of the **dry weight** of gray matter, 49–66 % of white matter, 78–81 % of myelin.
- **Check:** "60 % fat" is true only of dry weight (white matter). A living brain is mostly water. See the open question.

## 4. "One expert called the study a joke" (32–38 s)
- **Source:** Dr Dušan Materić (Helmholtz Centre for Environmental Research), interviewed by The Guardian, 13 Jan 2026: "The brain microplastic paper is a joke." His reason as reported: fat is known to give false positives for polyethylene.
- **Check:** the Guardian page could not be opened from here (blocked to automated readers); the quote is confirmed by Governing's "Who said that" (14 Jan 2026), which cites the Guardian, and other coverage.
- **On screen:** `D. Materić to The Guardian, Jan 2026`

## 5. "Plastic in artery plaque has been linked to heart attacks and strokes" (38–46 s)
- **Source:** Marfella R. et al., "Microplastics and Nanoplastics in Atheromas and Cardiovascular Events", *NEJM* 390, 900–910 (2024). <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2309822>
- **Numbers:** polyethylene detected in plaque of 150 of 257 patients (58.4 %); mean follow-up 33.7 months; composite of myocardial infarction, stroke or death from any cause: hazard ratio 4.53 (95 % CI 2.00–10.27). Observational: an association, not proof of cause.
- **On screen:** `Marfella et al., NEJM 2024 · observational`

## 6. "Netflix made a documentary" (10–15 s)
- **Check:** the Netflix film is *The Plastic Detox* (Louie Psihoyos and Josh Murphy), released March 2026, about plastics and fertility. It is not about the brain study, and it came out **after** the chemists' critique (Nov–Dec 2025) and the Guardian piece (Jan 2026), so "Netflix made a documentary. Then chemists looked at the method" gets the order wrong. See the open question.
