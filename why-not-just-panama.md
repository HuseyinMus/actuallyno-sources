## 1. The "easy" idea: a ditch about 80 km long
- **Length:** ACP, History of the Panama Canal: "The Panama Canal has a length of approximately 80 kilometers between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans." (UF gives 51 miles, deep water to deep water.)
- **Wording in the film:** "dig a ditch across eighty kilometers of Panama."
- **On screen:** `Panama Canal Authority`

## 2. France tried a sea-level canal (1881–1889), led by the man behind Suez
- **Start:** ACP, French Canal Construction: the first French engineering team "landed at Colon on January 29, 1881". (UF lists first ground broken on January 1, 1880, a ceremony; Linda Hall dates de Lesseps's visit to formally begin to December 1879.) The film says 1881, when the work crews arrived.
- **Sea level:** ACP: "the principal reason was de Lesseps' stubbornness in insisting on and sticking to the sea level plan." Linda Hall, The French Plan: the plan approved was "for a sea-level canal in Panama".
- **Suez:** Linda Hall Timeline, 1869: "Ferdinand de Lesseps finishes building the Suez Canal in Egypt." De Lesseps was the developer and promoter, not an engineer, so the film says "the man behind the Suez Canal".
- **End:** ACP: "by May 15, 1889, all activity on the Isthmus ceased", after the Compagnie Universelle was placed "under legal receivership". Linda Hall Timeline, 1889: de Lesseps "abandons his canal project". (ACP notes the French switched to a lock design in January 1888, too late. The film keeps to the sea-level plan that defined the attempt.)
- **On screen:** `ACP · Linda Hall Library`

## 3. Fail 1: the hills slid back into the cut whenever it rained
- **Source:** ACP, French Canal Construction: spoil "dump areas were too close to the excavation and slid back onto the channel whenever the rains came." Also: "The deeper the excavation, the worse the slides." And: "Ultimately, the unresolved problem of the slides would doom the sea level canal plan to failure."
- **Linda Hall, The French Attempt:** the French faced "inadequate equipment, landslides, floods, searing heat, and the ravages of malaria and yellow fever."
- **Mechanism (same as drawn):** Linda Hall, People Dying Every Day: "Gravity slides were caused by porous, saturated material sliding down slopes of firmer, more impervious rock or clay"; they "often happened in the wet season." The slides went on in the American era too (ACP, Culebra Cut: "Earth slides in Culebra Cut were a constant concern for construction engineers").
- **Wording in the film:** "Whenever the rains came, the soaked hills slid back into the cut."
- **On screen:** `ACP: slides at Culebra`

## 4. Fail 2: yellow fever and malaria killed thousands of workers
- **Linda Hall, The French Attempt:** "Workers lost even more with an estimated 20,000 dead." Linda Hall, Fighting Fever: "Over 22,000 workers died during the French effort." UF Interesting Facts: "Number of lives lost during French control 20,000."
- **ACP, French Canal Construction:** "Malaria, of course, continued to take even more lives than yellow fever." And "much of the death toll was never recorded."
- **Why the film says "thousands":** the totals are estimates, the toll was not fully recorded, and the sources differ (20,000 vs. over 22,000). "Thousands" is fully backed; the film shows no total. Not every death was from disease (accidents too), so the film says the fevers killed "thousands", not "20,000".
- **Respect:** a single cartoon mosquito over a calm, dimmed scene. No people, bodies or suffering are drawn.
- **On screen:** `est. 20,000+ died, French era · Linda Hall`

## 5. The real solution (United States, 1904–1914)
- **Start and finish:** ACP: "The beginning of the U.S. canal construction effort dates from May 4, 1904." Opening: August 15, 1914, with the transit of the SS Ancon (ACP, History in pictures; Linda Hall Timeline).
- **Mosquitoes first:** ACP, American Canal Construction: under Chief Sanitary Officer Dr. William Crawford Gorgas, "yellow fever was completely and permanently wiped out on the Isthmus", "with the last case reported in Panama City on November 11, 1905." Linda Hall, Fighting Fever: crews worked to "fumigate homes, put up screens, eliminate standing water, and spray drains and pools with oil." Malaria was reduced, not wiped out ("malaria cases, though much reduced, continued to be a problem"), so the film only says yellow fever was gone.
- **Not down to sea level, a lock canal instead:** Linda Hall, Locks: "Whether to build a sea-level canal or a system of locks had not been decided when the U.S. began digging in 1904." "In 1906, by a narrow vote, the Senate agreed" to locks. The Americans still dug the Culebra Cut (ACP), but only down to the lake's level, not to the sea.
- **A dammed river makes a lake 26 m up:** ACP, History: the locks raise ships "to the level of Gatun Lake (26 meters above sea level)". Linda Hall, Locks: "Gatun Lake, with a surface 85 feet above sea level". The lake was made by damming the Chagres River (Gatun Dam begun April 1907, Linda Hall Timeline).
- **Locks are water elevators, up and back down:** ACP: "The locks function as water lifts". Linda Hall, Locks: the lake fed "a system of locks to lift ships up from the sea to the lake", and locks at Pedro Miguel and Miraflores "raised and lowered ships on the Pacific side." UF: water flows "by gravity". The film draws three steps on each side (Gatun has three; on the Pacific side there is one at Pedro Miguel and two at Miraflores).
- **On screen:** `ACP: Gatun Lake, 26 m above sea level`

## 6. The twist: the Pacific end is east of the Atlantic end
- **Britannica**, "Panama Canal": the terminus near Balboa (Pacific) "is some 25 miles (40 km) east of its terminus near Colón" (Atlantic).
- **Coordinates check:** Colón ≈ 9.36° N, 79.90° W; Balboa ≈ 8.95° N, 79.57° W. The Pacific end is about 0.33° of longitude further east (≈ 36 km at this latitude). Sources give 22–25 miles; the film gives no number, only the direction.
- **Why:** the isthmus bends here, and the canal runs roughly north-west to south-east, so a ship heading from the Atlantic to the Pacific travels south-east.
- **Wording in the film:** "On the canal, the Pacific end is east of the Atlantic end."
- **On screen:** `Britannica · Colón 79.9° W, Balboa 79.6° W`

## Checked and dropped
- "Every rain" (draft): the source says "whenever the rains came" about spoil heaps and slopes; the film says "whenever the rains came".
- "America stopped trying to dig through the mountain" (draft): wrong, the Americans dug the Culebra Cut through the divide. The film says they stopped digging *down to sea level*.
- "The same man who built the Suez Canal" (draft): softened to "the man behind the Suez Canal" (de Lesseps led and promoted it; he was not its engineer).
- Death totals are not shown as a spoken number (see section 4).
