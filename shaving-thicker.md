# Video 12: Does shaving make hair grow back thicker?

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short on shaving and regrowth. Each claim is listed with its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen. Checked 2026-11-02.

## 1. The 1928 trial
- **Source:** Trotter M., "Hair growth and shaving", *The Anatomical Record* 37(4), 373–379 (February 1928). doi:10.1002/ar.1090370405 (bibliographic record via Crossref).
- **Check:** the publisher page refused automated access, so we could not read the paper itself. We cite it through the review below. Secondary summaries disagree on whether Trotter tested legs or the beard area, so the film says only "a 1928 trial" and does not show a body part for it.
- **Through the review:** "As early as 1928, a clinical trial showed that shaving had no effect on hair growth." (Vreeman & Carroll 2007, below)
- **On screen:** `Trotter, Anatomical Record 1928`

## 2. The 1970 leg study
- **Source:** Lynfield Y. L. & Macwilliams P., "Shaving and hair growth", *Journal of Investigative Dermatology* 55(3), 170–172 (1970). doi:10.1111/1523-1747.ep12280667, PMID 5459955.
- **Abstract:** "Five healthy young white men each shaved one leg weekly for several months and left the other leg as a control. No significant differences in total weight of hair produced in a measured area, or in width or rate of growth of individual hairs, could be ascribed to shaving."
- **On screen:** `Lynfield & Macwilliams, J Invest Dermatol 1970 · 5 men, one leg shaved`

## 3. The review and the twist
- **Source:** Vreeman R. C. & Carroll A. E., "Medical myths", *BMJ* 335(7633), 1288–1289 (2007). doi:10.1136/bmj.39420.420370.25. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2151163/>
- **Wording:**
  - "Strong scientific evidence disproves these claims."
  - "More recent studies confirm that shaving does not affect the thickness or rate of hair regrowth."
  - "shaving removes the dead portion of hair, not the living section lying below the skin's surface"
  - **Twist:** "Shaved hair lacks the finer taper seen at the ends of unshaven hair, giving an impression of coarseness."
  - **Colour:** "the new hair has not yet been lightened by the sun or other chemical exposures, resulting in an appearance that seems darker than existing hair."
- **Check:** the brief says shaving doesn't change "thickness, color or growth rate". The sources directly back **thickness (width) and growth rate** (Lynfield 1970, BMJ). For **colour**, the review explains why new hair *looks* darker, so the film says "looks darker: it hasn't been sun-faded yet", not "colour measured unchanged".
- **On screen:** `Vreeman & Carroll, BMJ 2007`
