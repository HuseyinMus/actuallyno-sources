# Video 11: Lightning never strikes twice?

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short on the saying "lightning never strikes the same place twice". Each claim is listed with its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen. Checked 2026-11-02.

## 1. Roy Sullivan: struck 7 times
- **Source:** Guinness World Records, "Most lightning strikes survived". <https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/most-lightning-strikes-survived>
  - "The only man in the world to be struck by lightning seven times was ex-park ranger Roy C. Sullivan, the human lightning conductor of Virginia, USA."
  - Strikes listed: "1942 (lost big toe nail)", "1969 (lost eyebrows)", "July 1970 (left shoulder seared)", "16 April 1972 (hair set on fire)", "7 August 1973 (new hair re-fired and legs seared)", "5 June 1976 (ankle injured)", "25 June 1977 (chest and stomach burns)".
- **Source:** Guinness World Records news, "Incredible story of man who survived being struck by lightning SEVEN times" (Vicki Newman, 26 January 2023): "He was working as a ranger at Shenandoah National Park in the Blue Ridge Mountains". <https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/1/incredible-story-of-man-who-survived-being-struck-by-lightning-seven-times-733932>
- **Checks:**
  - (a) The strikes were not witnessed, only their aftermath. Some writers doubt parts of the accounts, so the film says "Guinness counts 7", not "science measured 7".
  - (b) Seven strikes over 35 years in different places (a lookout tower, a truck, a garden, a boat) are about a person, not one spot. The film's point is about places: the person story is the hook, and the twist is the building.
  - (c) His death is not mentioned in the film.
- **On screen:** `Guinness World Records · Roy C. Sullivan, 1942–1977`

## 2. The same place, many times a year
- **Source:** U.S. National Weather Service, "Lightning Myths". <https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-myths>
  - Myth: "Lightning never strikes the same place twice."
  - Fact: "Lightning often strikes the same place repeatedly, especially if it's a tall, pointy, isolated object. The Empire State Building is hit an average of 23 times a year".
- **Check:** other sources give 25 or even about 100 strikes a year. The film uses the NWS figure, "about 23 times a year".
- **On screen:** `National Weather Service: about 23 strikes a year`

## 3. Not used
- "Up to 100 a year" (U.S. Air Force fact sheet) is a much higher figure and conflicts with NWS and NOAA. It is not used.
- Odds of being struck are not used, because the figures vary by source and by year.

## 4. Hook and script claims
- **Hook (used):** "You think lightning never strikes twice? Guinness says it hit this man 7 times. But why isn't he the worst case?" The "worst case" loop closes at the reveal of the building that NWS says is hit about 23 times a year (section 2). That is more strikes on one place every year than Guinness counts for one person in 35 years.
- **"A watchtower. His truck. His garden. A boat."**: from the Guinness news article, which describes the strikes while fleeing a watchtower, driving his truck, in his garden and on a fishing boat.
- **"lightning loves tall, pointy, isolated things"**: NWS ("especially if it's a tall, pointy, isolated object").
