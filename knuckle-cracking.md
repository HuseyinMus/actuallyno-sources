# Video 14: Does cracking your knuckles cause arthritis?

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short on knuckle cracking. This is the English adaptation of the Turkish research in [tr/parmak-citlatma.md](tr/parmak-citlatma.md). Each claim is listed with its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen. Abstracts re-read via Europe PMC on 2026-11-03.

## 1. Donald Unger's one-man experiment
- **Primary source:** Unger D. L., "Does knuckle cracking lead to arthritis of the fingers?", *Arthritis & Rheumatism* 41(5), 949–950 (1998). doi:10.1002/1529-0131(199805)41:5<949::AID-ART36>3.0.CO;2-3, PMID 9588755. It is a letter with no abstract.
- **Access note:** the full text is paywalled at Wiley (HTTP 403 to us, checked again 2026-11-03). Details of the letter are therefore taken from the sources below, not from the letter itself.
- **Ig Nobel Prize in Medicine 2009,** official citation (improbable.com/ig/winners, read 2026-11-03): "Donald L. Unger, of Thousand Oaks, California, USA, for investigating a possible cause of arthritis of the fingers, by diligently cracking the knuckles of his left hand — but never cracking the knuckles of his right hand — every day for more than sixty (60) years." The same entry cites the 1998 letter.
- **Duration conflict:** the Ig Nobel citation says "more than sixty years". Secondary accounts of the 1998 letter say 50 years, and the often-quoted 36,500 cracks equals exactly 2 × 365 × 50. The film therefore says **"over fifty years"**, which is consistent with both.
- **Result (secondary accounts; the letter could not be read):** no arthritis in either hand, and no apparent difference between the hands. We could not confirm whether the hands were checked by X-ray or by examination, so the film does **not** say Unger used X-rays, and says he "reported" the result.
- **36,500:** appears only in secondary sources, so the film does not show it.
- **"A doctor":** the letter is signed "Donald L. Unger, MD", per secondary accounts.
- **Limit:** one person (n = 1), not blinded. The film says "that's one man."
- **On screen:** `Unger, Arthritis Rheum 1998 · one man · 50+ years` and `Ig Nobel Medicine 2009`

## 2. The X-ray study
- **Source:** deWeber K., Olszewski M. & Ortolano R., "Knuckle cracking and hand osteoarthritis", *Journal of the American Board of Family Medicine* 24(2), 169–174 (2011). doi:10.3122/jabfm.2011.02.100156, PMID 21383216.
- **Method:** "a retrospective case-control study among persons aged 50 to 89 years who received a radiograph of the right hand". "Patients had radiographically proven hand OA, and controls did not."
- **Sample:** "215 respondents (135 patients, 80 controls)". "The prevalence of KC among 215 respondents … was 20%."
- **Result:** "the prevalence of OA in any joint was similar among those who crack knuckles (18.1%) and those who do not (21.5%; P = .548)." Conclusion: habitual knuckle cracking "does not seem to be a risk factor for hand OA."
- **Wording in the film:** "Crackers didn't have more arthritis." The film does not say cracking protects the joints: the 18.1% vs 21.5% gap is not significant (P = .548).
- **On screen:** `deWeber et al., J Am Board Fam Med 2011 · 215 people, X-rays` and the two bars `18.1%` / `21.5%`.

## 3. The honest catch: swelling and grip
- **Source:** Castellanos J. & Axelrod D., "Effect of habitual knuckle cracking on hand function", *Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases* 49(5), 308–309 (1990). doi:10.1136/ard.49.5.308, PMID 2344210, PMC1004074.
- **Sample:** "300 consecutive patients aged 45 years or above" (74 habitual knuckle crackers, 226 non-crackers).
- **Arthritis:** "There was no increased preponderance of arthritis of the hand in either group".
- **But:** "habitual knuckle crackers were more likely to have hand swelling and lower grip strength." No figures are given in the abstract.
- **The authors' own conclusion** is stronger than ours: "habitual knuckle cracking results in functional hand impairment." It is a cross-sectional study, and cracking was also "associated with manual labour, biting of the nails, smoking, and drinking alcohol", so the film says **"linked to"**, not "causes".
- **Wording in the film:** "a 1990 study linked the habit to swollen hands and a weaker grip."
- **On screen:** `Castellanos & Axelrod, Ann Rheum Dis 1990 · 300 patients`

## 4. What the pop is
- **Source:** Kawchuk G. N., Fryer J., Jaremko J. L., Zeng H., Rowe L. & Thompson R., "Real-time visualization of joint cavitation", *PLoS ONE* 10(4), e0119470 (2015). doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0119470, PMID 25875374, PMC4398549.
- **Method:** ten finger (metacarpophalangeal) joints, pulled by a cable, filmed with real-time MRI "at a rate of 3.2 frames per second until the cracking event occurred."
- **Finding:** "joint cracking is associated with cavity inception rather than collapse of a pre-existing bubble." The cavity "remained visible" after the sound, consistent with "sustained gas cavities".
- **Wording in the film:** the MRI caught "a gas cavity forming" in the joint, "not a bubble bursting."
- **On screen:** `Kawchuk et al., PLOS ONE 2015 · real-time MRI`

## Hook, narration and on-screen numbers
- **Hook:** "Before you crack your knuckles again: one doctor tested it on himself for over fifty years. Left hand only. Why?" "One doctor", "over fifty years" and "left hand only" are from section 1 (Ig Nobel citation; secondary accounts of the 1998 letter). The hook makes no health claim; the cost it hints at is the grip and swelling link in section 3.
- **Narration:** "He reported no arthritis in either one. But that's one man." (section 1) · "215 hand X-rays. Crackers didn't have more arthritis." (section 2) · "a gas cavity forming in the joint. Not a bubble bursting." (section 4) · "a 1990 study linked the habit to swollen hands and a weaker grip. Arthritis? Probably not. Grip? Maybe." (section 3)
- **Numbers on screen:** 50+ years (1), 215 people (2), 18.1% vs 21.5% (2), 300 patients (3), 1990, 2011, 2015 and 1998 (the citations).
- **Health wording:** "linked to", "may", "probably". Nothing here is medical advice.
