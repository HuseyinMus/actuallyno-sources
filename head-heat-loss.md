# Video 13: Do you lose most of your body heat through your head?

Sources for the *Actually, No* Short on head heat loss. Each claim is listed with its primary source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen. Checked 2026-11-02.

## 1. The head-submersion experiment
- **Source:** Pretorius T., Bristow G. K., Steinman A. M. & Giesbrecht G. G., "Thermal effects of whole head submersion in cold water on nonshivering humans", *Journal of Applied Physiology* 101(2), 669–675 (2006). doi:10.1152/japplphysiol.01241.2005, PMID 16614357. Abstract read via Europe PMC; the full paper is not open access.
- **Abstract wording:**
  - "Eight healthy male subjects were studied in 17 degrees C water"
  - "Submersion of the head (7% of the body surface area) in the body-exposed conditions increased total heat loss by only 10%."
  - "In 17 degrees C water, the head does not contribute relatively more than the rest of the body to surface heat loss"
  - "head submersion increased core cooling rate much more (average of 42%) than it increased total heat loss. This may be explained by a redistribution of blood flow in response to stimulation of thermosensitive and/or trigeminal receptors in the scalp, neck and face"
- **On screen:** `Pretorius et al., J Appl Physiol 2006`

## 2. The review and where the myth came from
- **Source:** Vreeman R. C. & Carroll A. E., "Festive medical myths", *BMJ* 337:a2769 (2008). doi:10.1136/bmj.a2769, PMID 19091758. The article is paywalled (403 to automated access, not in PMC), so it is cited through the authors' own university release:
- **Indiana University School of Medicine news release, "Medical Myths for the Holiday Season: True, False or Unproven?", 18 December 2008.** <https://medicine.iu.edu/news/2008/12/medical-myths-for-the-holiday-season-true-false-or-unproven>
  - "They believe this myth likely originated with an old military study where scientists put subjects in arctic survival suits without hats and measured their heat loss in cold temperatures."
  - "They did lose the most heat through their heads, but only because it was the only bare part of their body."
  - "the subjects would not have lost more than 10 percent of their body heat through their heads."
  - "Any uncovered part of the body loses heat and will drop the core body temperature proportionally"
  - "the head does not require special attention."
- **On screen:** `Vreeman & Carroll, BMJ 2008`

## 3. The army field manual figure (NOT YET VERIFIED)
- The brief cites "a 1970s US Army field manual, about 40–45%". We have not found the manual's own text.
- Secondary sources agree on the 40–45% figure but not on the date. Cleveland Clinic says "an old U.S. Army Field Manual". MeatEater dates it to the 1950s. FM 31-70, *Basic Cold Weather Manual*, had 1959 and 1968 editions.
- **Until the primary text is found:** the film does not show the manual, a year or 40–45%. It uses the arctic-suit experiment (section 2) as the origin instead.

## Hook, narration and on-screen numbers
- **Hook:** "You think you lose half your body heat through your head? Your head is only about 7% of your skin." The "half" is the belief being checked, not a claim. The 7% is the head's share of body surface area (section 1).
- **"No more than about 10%":** the IU release (section 2) for the arctic-suit case, and "increased total heat loss by only 10%" in Pretorius 2006 (section 1).
- **Twist:** "your core cooled faster" is Pretorius's 42% average rise in core cooling rate (section 1). The film says a hat "may still help", not that it prevents anything.
- **Origin:** the film shows a generic "old military study" with arctic suits and no hats (section 2). It shows no manual, year or 40–45% figure (section 3).
- **On-screen extras:** "17 °C water · 8 men" and "cooling rate +42% (avg)" are from the Pretorius 2006 abstract (section 1).
