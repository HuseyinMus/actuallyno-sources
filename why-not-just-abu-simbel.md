# Video 19: Why not just build a wall around Abu Simbel?

Sources for this *Why not just...?* Short on the *Actually, No* channel. Each claim is listed with its source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen. Sources read on 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12.

Main sources:
- **Berg 1978**: Lennart Berg (VBB, the Swedish consulting engineers for the whole salvage), "The Salvage of the Abu Simbel Temples", *Monumentum* vol. 17, ICOMOS, 1978: https://www.icomos.org/public/monumentum/vol17/vol17_2.pdf (read in full as text).
- **Crimson 1963**: "Abu Simbel", *The Harvard Crimson*, 25 November 1963 (a contemporary account of the proposals and funding): https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1963/11/25/abu-simbel-psince-1250-bc-the/
- **Atlas Copco**: "Abu Simbel – Unparalleled relocation project" (the tool supplier; Sandvik hand saws): https://www.atlascopco.com/en-eg/history-in-egypt/landmark-projects-abu-simbel
- **Wikipedia**, "Abu Simbel" (read 2026-11-11): used only to find primary references (UNESCO Courier, March 1980; the VBB Concluding Report), never as the source of a claim.

## 1. The problem: the Aswan High Dam would drown the temples
- Berg 1978: construction of the High Dam "was started in 1960. From 1964 on, the water level of the Nile was to be gradually raised", creating Lake Nasser "with a maximum water level some 60 metres higher than previously".
- Berg 1978: if flooded, the temples "would soon have deteriorated, since the sandstone in the temple region suffers considerable losses in strength and durability when submerged."
- Age: Crimson 1963: "Since 1250 B.C., the seated colossi of Abu Simbel have stared fixedly across the Nile". So the temples are over 3,000 years old.
- **Wording in the film:** "Your dam will flood Abu Simbel, 3,000 years old." / "Here's the catch. Egypt's new High Dam would raise the Nile about 60 meters."
- **On screen:** `Built c. 1250 BC · Harvard Crimson, 1963`, `Berg/VBB 1978 (ICOMOS): lake ~60 m higher`

## 2. Fail 1: a wall (dam) around the temples
- Crimson 1963: "The French proposed building a dam to protect it from the Aswan lake"; "their plan would have required constant pumping to remove seepage from the pourous sandstone under Abu Simbel." "And it would have cost at least $80 million."
- **Wording in the film:** "But the temple sits on porous sandstone. A wall leaks underneath, so you'd pump seepage forever. At least 80 million dollars."
- **On screen:** `Harvard Crimson, Nov 1963: dam plan, $80M+`

## 3. Fail 2: lift the whole temple on jacks
- Crimson 1963: "A cheaper, more practical, but seemingly fantastic Italian proposition was accepted by UNESCO in 1961." "Then, 300 synchronized hydraulic jacks would begin to raise the temple, one-sixteenth of an inch at a time." "It cost $70,000,000"; donors "were unwilling to give much money"; "UNESCO collected little over $7.5 million in three years".
- **Wording in the film:** "Okay, then lift the whole temple! Italy's plan: 300 jacks, a 16th of an inch at a time." / "70 million dollars. In 3 years, the world chipped in about 7.5." (UNESCO's collection, per the Crimson.) The film does not say the jacks plan failed technically; it was dropped for a cheaper plan.
- **On screen:** `Harvard Crimson, Nov 1963: jacks plan, $70M`

## 4. The real solution: cut it up and rebuild it higher (1964–1968)
- The plan: Berg 1978: "a project scheme as elaborated by VBB was chosen"; "The basic idea of the scheme was to have the temple façades and walls cut into big blocks, which would be transported to a safe place above the highest future water level, where they would be rebuilt." Crimson 1963: "The Swedes proposed cutting the temple and statues into sections"; "The whole process should cost about $36 million."
- Blocks: Berg 1978: façade blocks were "not to exceed 30 tons", room blocks "limited to 20 tons". (The total number of blocks is given as 1,035, 1,036 or 1,042 in secondary sources; the film says "blocks", no count.)
- Saws: Berg 1978: "Cutting in the visible temple surfaces, where the cuts had to be very thin (maximum 8 mm), was done practically exclusively by means of hand saws"; the rest of each cut was made from behind with "motor-driven chainsaws". Atlas Copco: "sawed into blocks with handsaws from Sandvik".
- While they worked: Berg 1978: "a cofferdam had to be built in front of the Temples", with "continuous pumping" during dismantling (a TEMPORARY wall, 1964–1966).
- How far: Berg 1978: "The horizontal transfer of the two temples was 208 metres… Vertically, the Great Temple was lifted 65 metres and the Small Temple 67 metres."
- Same direction: Berg 1978: "the orientation of each temple in relation to the cardinal points was also kept exactly the same as on the old sites." Atlas Copco: so that "the rays of the sun would continue to illuminate the statues in the larger temple's inner halls, 60 meters into the cliff."
- Dates: work on site from 1964 (Berg 1978); concluded "on September 22, 1968" (Atlas Copco).
- **Wording in the film:** "They cut the temple into blocks, up to 30 tons each, with hand saws on every visible face." / "They rebuilt the whole thing 65 meters higher, facing exactly the same way, so the sun still reaches the statues inside."
- **On screen:** `Berg/VBB 1978: blocks ≤ 30 t, hand saws`, `Berg/VBB 1978: 65 m up, 208 m back, same facing`

## 5. The twist: the mountain behind it is a concrete dome
- Berg 1978: "the rock masses of the hills above the temples were prevented from resting directly on these by dome-shaped concrete structures, one over each temple." "The dome over the Great Temple has a free span of about 60 m. and a height of 25 m. Carrying in all about 100,000 metric tons in a single span".
- Berg 1978: "The inside of the great dome can however still be seen"; "For tourists visiting Abu Simbel, the great dome has become an extra attraction."
- Atlas Copco: "Gigantic cupolas where thereafter constructed above the temple roofs, and surplus stone masses… were formed into natural-appearing hills above the cupolas."
- **Wording in the film:** "The mountain behind it is fake. It's a giant concrete dome, and you can walk inside it."
- **On screen:** `Berg/VBB 1978: dome span ~60 m`

## Checked and dropped
- "The sun festival moved by one day after the move": only travel sites say it; not used.
- Number of blocks: sources differ (1,035 / 1,036 / 1,042), so no count is used.
- Final cost: sources differ ($36 million estimate in 1963; $40–41.7 million later; other figures), so no final cost is used.
