# Video 16: Why not just make airplane windows square?

Sources for the second *Why not just...?* Short on the *Actually, No* channel. Each claim is listed with its source, the quoted wording, and the short credit shown on screen. Read on 2026-11-09.

**Main primary source:** Ministry of Transport and Civil Aviation, *Civil Aircraft Accident: Report of the Court of Inquiry into the Accidents to Comet G-ALYP on 10th January, 1954, and Comet G-ALYY on 8th April, 1954* (C.A.P. 127, HMSO, 1955; signed 1 February 1955). Read as the scanned copy hosted by the Aviation Safety Network (asn.flightsafety.org/reports/1954/19540408_COMT_G-ALYY.pdf). Paragraph numbers below are the report's own (Report on G-ALYP).

## 1. A pressurised cabin goes through a cycle every flight
- **Report, para. 62–63:** the RAE test reproduced "a series of pressurised flights": "the cabin and wings were repeatedly subjected to a cycle of loading as far as possible equivalent to that to which they would be subjected in the period between take-off and landing", including "one application of cabin pressure".
- **Para. 64:** the test aircraft G-ALYU "had made 1,230 pressurised flights before the test".
- **Wording in the film:** "Every flight, the cabin is pumped up, then let down. Up, down, thousands of times." (G-ALYU had 1,230 real pressurised flights plus 1,830 in the tank, so "thousands" is supported.)

## 2. Stress piles up at sharp corners
- **Report, para. 68:** "it now seemed highly probable that the stress near the corners of the windows was higher than had been believed by the designers".
- **Para. 69:** the highest stress in the skin was at "the edge near the corner of the window", "probably over 40,000 lb./sq. in." at the working pressure, "significantly higher than had been previously believed".
- **Para. 79:** "the fundamental cause of the failure was the existence around the corners of the windows and other cut-outs of a level of stress higher than is consistent with a long life of the cabin".
- **Para. 125:** the court discusses "stress concentrations" at the corner of the window.
- **Why a rounder corner helps (general engineering):** Inglis, C. E., "Stresses in a plate due to the presence of cracks and sharp corners", *Transactions of the Institution of Naval Architects* 55 (1913): for an elliptical hole the peak stress is σ_max = σ(1 + 2√(a/ρ)), so it rises sharply as the tip radius ρ gets smaller. A sharper corner concentrates more stress; a rounder one spreads it.
- **Wording in the film:** "And sharp corners hate that. Stress piles up at the corner, and a tiny crack starts to grow." · "The fix: round the corners, so the stress spreads out."
- **On screen:** `Court of Inquiry, 1955` and `Inglis, 1913`

## 3. The Comet: the first jet airliner
- **Guinness World Records,** "First aircraft, commercial jet-powered" (read 2026-11-09): the British de Havilland Comet 1, which "first flew on 27 July 1949" and made its first scheduled passenger flight on "2 May 1952".
- **Report, para. 11 and 13:** the first prototype flew on 27 July 1949; the passenger service "was actually commenced on the 2nd May, 1952".
- **Wording in the film:** "It brought down the first jet airliner."

## 4. Two Comets broke up in flight in 1954
- **Report, covering letter (1 Feb 1955):** inquiries "into the causes and circumstances of two accidents which occurred in the Mediterranean to Comet aircraft, the first on the 10th January, 1954, to G-ALYP, the second on the 8th April, 1954, to G-ALYY".
- **Report on G-ALYP, header:** place "Over the Mediterranean off Elba"; crew of 6, "Killed"; "PASSENGERS: 29—All Killed".
- **Para. 95:** the court accepted "the main conclusion of the Report that the cause of the accident to Yoke Peter was the structural failure of the pressure cabin brought about by fatigue".
- **Para. 124:** refers to "the accidents to Yoke Peter at Elba or Yoke Yoke at Naples".
- **Not in the film:** the G-ALYY casualty count (its section of the report was not read), so the film gives no death toll for either crash. It says "two Comets broke up in flight over the Mediterranean".
- **Tone:** a stylized plane and a growing crack only. No breakup, fire or people.
- **On screen:** `Court of Inquiry, 1955`

## 5. The water tank test
- **Report, para. 62:** loading the cabin "by immersing the whole cabin in a tank, and filling the tank and the cabin simultaneously with water. Pressure in the cabin is then raised by pumping in water".
- **Para. 63:** the tests on G-ALYU (Yoke Uncle) began early in June 1954 at the RAE.
- **Para. 64:** "Yoke Uncle had made 1,230 pressurised flights before the test and after the equivalent of a further 1,830 such flights, making a total of 3,060, the cabin structure failed, the starting point of the failure being the corner of one of the cabin windows".
- **Wording in the film:** "So investigators put a whole Comet cabin in a giant water tank, and pumped it up and down. After 3,060 flights in total, it split open at a window corner."
- **On screen:** `RAE tank test · Court of Inquiry, 1955`, number `3,060`.

## 6. The twist: the Elba crack started at a roof window, not a passenger window
- **Report, para. 73:** RAE received "a piece of cabin skin" from the top of the cabin; "It contained the two windows in which lie the aerials of the A.D.F. (Automatic Direction Finding) equipment."
- **Para. 75:** "it was established that it was here that the first fracture of the cabin structure of Yoke Peter occurred".
- **Para. 76:** "I accept R.A.E.'s conclusion that the first fracture of the cabin occurred near the rear A.D.F. window and spread fore and aft from it."
- **Para. 77:** "it is probable that it started near the starboard aft corner of the rear A.D.F. window, at a point where examination by experts showed that fatigue had existed, at the edge of the countersunk hole through which a bolt passed".
- **The popular version:** many retellings say the Comet's square passenger windows caused the crashes. For the Elba aircraft the court placed the origin at the roof ADF window corner. (The tank-test failure did start at the corner of a cabin window, para. 64.)
- **Wording in the film:** "But here's the twist. On the plane lost near Elba, the first crack started at a small window in the roof, for a radio aerial. Even the famous story is a bit off. Same lesson: corners kill."
- **On screen:** `Court of Inquiry, 1955, para. 76–77`

## Not claimed
- No casualty numbers on screen. No claim about the exact window shape of later Comets or other airliners. No claim that the windows were "square" (the report speaks of window corners).
